# 13 Learning to learn

The promotion of learner autonomy is a fundamental objective of the communicative approach adopted by the Council of Europe. In moving towards the Vantage level, responsibility is progressively transferred from teacher to learner, who must develop the insights, skills, and attitudes required to function autonomously.

## The learning-to-learn objective

### I Concerning needs and objectives
1. The learners are aware of the nature of their communicative needs.
2. The learners are aware of the nature of the learning objective offered to them.
3. The learners have insight into the degree of relevance of the learning objective to their communicative needs.
4. In topic-related tasks and lexicon, the learners are able to identify, learn and use terms specific to their own needs and interests.
5. The learners can identify those of their communication needs, if any, which are not catered for in the general objective.
6. The learners can describe, in general terms, additional objectives which will satisfy these unique needs (*I'd like to read articles about economics*, *I'd like to write business letters*).

### II Concerning learning processes
1. The learners are familiar with the possibility of dividing an overall learning task into a number of sub-tasks, each with its own objective.
2. The learners are familiar with the distinctions between productive ability and receptive ability and with the differing degrees of skill required.
3. The learners are aware of the contributions of pragmatic, grammatical, lexical and phonological adequacy to communicative effectiveness.
4. The learners can identify the roles (acquisition of knowledge, insight, or skill) of various types of learning materials.
5. The learners know how to find information about usage (e.g. in dictionaries, reference works, and reference grammars).
6. The learners have experienced various methods of vocabulary acquisition and have identified which ones are particularly useful to themselves.
7. The learners are aware of the potential of learning through exposure to foreign language use and know how compensation strategies enable them to cope with unknown elements.

### III Concerning learning from direct experience of the language
1. The learners are able to engage in communicative interaction using Vantage-level resources and learn from experience.
2. The learners are able to observe the language and strategies used by a more experienced interlocutor and thus increase their own repertory.
3. The learners are able as listeners and readers to perceive, memorise and note down words and expressions not previously encountered, noting their context and value.
4. The learners are able to repeat back new words and expressions which occur in conversations and integrate them into their notes and repertory.
5. The learners are able to experiment with forms of expression (recombining known structures/rules), note their acceptance or non-acceptance by more experienced speakers, and modify their internal rules accordingly.
6. The learners are able to employ compensation and repair strategies, learning and using new language supplied by their interlocutor.

### IV Concerning evaluation
1. The learners can monitor their progress towards the terminal objective by relating their ability to successive intermediate objectives.
2. The learners are aware of the role of formative assessment as an aid to the planning of further learning activities.
