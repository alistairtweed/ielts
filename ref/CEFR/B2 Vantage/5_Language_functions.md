# 5 Language functions

## Introduction
The Vantage learner, like the Threshold learner, wishes to use a language (English) to transact the business of daily living and enter into relations with other speakers. While both levels cover a similar broad functional range, the Vantage learner utilizes a richer set of exponents, a more sensitive sub-categorisation of functions (especially for emotional reactions and discussions), and more developed structural variables to express fine shades of meaning.

## 1. Imparting and Seeking Information
* **1.1 Identifying and Specifying**: Pointing out people or objects.
  * *Exponents*: `this (one)/that (one)/these/those; him/her/them`; `the (adj) one + adjunct phrase/relative clause` (e.g., *the small one with the blue buttons*); `It's me/you/him/her/us/them/NP` (e.g., *It's the postman.*); `Pronoun/NP + be + NP` (e.g., *This is the bedroom.*, *The animals over there are my dogs.*).
* **1.2 Stating and Reporting (Describing, Narrating)**: 
  * *Exponents*: Sequences of declarative sentences; `NP + say, think + complement clause` (*He says the shop is shut.*); `NP + ask, wonder + indirect question` (*He asked where they were going.*); `there + be + NP + adjunct` (*There is a bank on the corner.*).
* **1.3 Correcting**: 
  * Use of contrastive stress.
  * **1.3.1 Correcting a positive statement**: `No + tag` with fall-rise intonation (*No, it isn't.*); negative sentence + corrected positive sentence (*Valetta isn't in Italy. It's in Malta.*).
  * **1.3.2 Correcting a negative statement**: `Yes + tag` (*Yes, you did.*); positive statement with fall-rise on auxiliary (*You did go to London.*).
* **1.4 Asking**:
  * **1.4.1 Asking for confirmation/denial**: Interrogative sentences with low rising intonation (*Did you see him?*); *Please (can) you tell me whether...*
  * **1.4.2 Demanding confirmation/denial**: Interrogative sentences with low falling intonation + *yes or no* (*Did you see him, yes or no?*).
  * **1.4.3 Expecting confirmation**: Positive statement + negative tag rising (*You saw him, didn't you?*); Negative statement + positive tag rising.
  * **1.4.4 Demanding confirmation**: Positive statement + negative tag falling (*You saw him, didn't you?*).
  * **1.4.5 Querying a statement**: Repeating statement with high rise/fall-rise (*You saw him?*); question tag with high rise/fall-rise (*Didn't you?*); *Are you quite sure (about that)?*
  * **1.4.6 Asking for information**: Wh-questions (*what happened?, when?, where?, how?, how + adj/adv, why?*); *Wh is it + complement clause* (*When is it your train leaves?*); *(Please) (can you) tell me + NP/wh-clause*.
  * **1.4.7 Seeking identification**: *which?*, *who?*, *what?* (for occupation/nationality), *whose?*.
  * **1.4.8 Asking for specification**: *what sort/kind of NP*.
  * **1.4.9 Expressing curiosity**: *I wonder...*, *I'd like to know...*.
* **1.5 Answering Questions**:
  * **1.5.1 Confirming/Disconfirming**: *Yes/No*, *I (don't) think so*, *I believe so/not*, expressions of agreement/disagreement, probability, ignorance, forgetting, certainty.
  * **1.5.2 Giving information**: Short answers using temporal, locative, manner, degree, or reason phrases/clauses.
  * **1.5.3 Identifying**: References to people, occupations (*She's an actress.*), possessors (*They're mine.*), or things.
  * **1.5.4 Specifying**: *It's a grey squirrel.*
  * **1.5.5 Expressions of ignorance**: *I haven't a clue.*

## 2. Expressing and Finding Out Attitudes
* **2.1 Attitudes to Matters of Fact**:
  * **2.1.1 Expressing agreement**: Strong (*Exactly!, Absolutely!, Well said!, You can say that again.*); Positive (*Yes, we do.*, *That's right.*); Negative (*No, we can't.*, *Certainly not.*); Reluctant (*If you say so.*, *I suppose so.*); Conceding (*Good point.*, *Fair enough.*); With reservations (*Up to a point, yes.*, *That may well be so, but...*); Demurring (*Well.*).
  * **2.1.2 Expressing disagreement**: *I don't agree.*, *That's not right.*, *You're wrong.*; Strong (*Absolute nonsense/rubbish.*, *I couldn't agree less.*, *No way!*); Weak (*I can't altogether agree.*, *I'm not so sure.*).
  * **2.1.3 Enquiring about agreement**: *Do you agree?*, *What do you think?*
  * **2.1.4 Inviting agreement**: Tag questions with falling intonation (*She is French, isn't she?*); *Don't you agree/think...?*
  * **2.1.5 Inviting disagreement**: *Surely you don't think...?*
  * **2.1.6 Denying statements**: *That isn't true.*, *I deny it.*, *That is a downright lie.*, *Not at all.*
* **2.2 Expressing Knowledge, Memory, Belief**:
  * **2.2.1 Expressing knowledge**: *I (don't) know (where he lives / it's cold).*
  * **2.2.2 Asking about knowledge**: *Do you know about...?*, *Have you ever heard of...?*, *You know..., don't you?*
  * **2.2.3 Asserting ignorance**: *I've no idea.*, *I haven't a clue.*
  * **2.2.4 Expressing remembering/forgetting**: *I (don't) remember + NP / gerund / to + inf / wh-clause*; *I forget whether...*; *I've (never) forgotten...*
  * **2.2.5 Enquiring about remembering/forgetting**: *Do(n't) you remember...?*, *Have(n't) you forgotten to...?*
  * **2.2.6 Reminding someone**: *Don't forget to...*, *Please remember that...*
  * **2.2.7 Degrees of certainty**: Confident assertion (*certainly, definitely, undoubtedly, beyond any doubt*, *I am quite certain*, stressed auxiliary); Tentative assertion (*seem, appear, look as if, perhaps, maybe, I think/believe so*, *I could be wrong, but...*); Uncertainty (*I don't know if...*, *I'm in two minds.*); Doubt/incredulity (*I can't believe...*, *I rather doubt if...*); Bewilderment (*I'm completely baffled.*); Enquiring about certainty (*Are you sure?*).
* **2.3 Expressing Modality**:
  * Probability (*must be Belgian*, *likely to see him*, *may/might/could*); Necessity (*necessary, must/cannot*, logical deduction); Denying necessity (*It isn't necessarily so*, *need not*); Obligation (*have to, must*); Denying obligation (*needn't*); Ability (*can, be able to*); Denying ability (*cannot, unable*); Permissibility (*allowed, permitted, smoking is allowed*); Denying permissibility (*not allowed, prohibited, strictly prohibited, must not, not supposed to*); Enquiring about permissibility (*Where may one park?*).
* **2.4 Expressing and Enquiring about Volition**:
  * Wishes/Wants/Desires (*I'd like + NP*, *I want*, *I wish I were*); Negative wishes (*I don't want any trouble*, *I wish I weren't so fat*); Enquiring about wishes (*What would you like?*, *Would you like to come fishing?*); Intentions (*going to*, *will*, *intend to*, *thinking of*); Negative intentions (*not going to*, *won't*, *don't intend to*); Enquiring about intentions (*What are you going to do?*).
* **2.5 Expressing and Enquiring about Emotions**:
  * Pleasure/Happiness (*lovely, wonderful, great, fine*, *How nice!*, *I'm delighted*); Unhappiness/Sadness (*Oh dear!*, *miserable, depressed, gloomy, sad*); Enquiring about health/emotions (*How are you feeling?*); Cause of unhappiness (*What's the matter?*); Exhorting (*Cheer up!*, *It's not the end of the world!*, *Never despair!*, *Never mind!*); Regret/Sympathy (*What a shame/pity*, *I'm so very sorry*); Fellow-feeling/Empathy (*I know just how you feel.*, *I fully sympathise.*); Hope/Expectation (*I do hope so*, *I am looking forward to*); Disappointment (*I feel very disappointed.*); Fear/Anxiety (*Help!*, *scared to death*, *I'm afraid*, *worried*); Enquiring about fear (*Are you worried about...?*, *Is there something on your mind?*); Pain/Anguish (*Ow!, Ouch!*, *That hurts!*, *in great pain*, *My leg is hurting*, *headache*); Enquiring about pain (*Does it hurt?, Where is the pain?*); Reassuring (*Don't worry.*, *It's going to be all right.*, *Relax.*, *Keep calm.*); Relief (*Whew!*, *Thank goodness!*, *What a relief!*); Liking/Affection (*very good, pleasant*, *I enjoy*, *fond of*, *I love*); Dislike (*Ugh!*, *horrible, awful*, *I hate, detest*, *I simply can't stand*); Enquiring about likes (*What do you think of...?*, *Who is your favourite...?*); Preference (*I prefer X to Y*, *I'd rather*, *like better than*); Enquiring about preference (*Which do you prefer?*, *Coffee or tea?*, *Black or white?*); Satisfaction (*Good!, Fine!, Excellent!*, *Just right!*, *just what I wanted*, *That'll do.*); Dissatisfaction (*This soup is cold.*, *I'm not satisfied*, *This is not good enough.*); Enquiring about satisfaction (*Is everything all right?*, *Will this do?*, *Is anything wrong?*); Complaining (*I have a complaint to make.*, *I'm sorry, but...*); Bad temper (*I'm in a bad mood.*, *livid, annoyed, irritated*); Reacting to bad temper (*Calm down!*, *Don't get worked up!*, *storm in a teacup*, *mountain out of a molehill*); Apologising for temper (*I'm sorry I lost my temper.*, *No hard feelings.*); Interest (*Fascinating!*, *How very interesting!*, *interested in*); Lack of interest (*How boring!*, *utterly bored*, *not at all interested*, *leaves me cold*); Surprise (*Good Heavens!*, *What a nice surprise!*, *How surprising!*, *Just fancy that!*, *complete shock*); Enquiring about surprise (*Doesn't that surprise you?*); Lack of surprise (*Well?*, *Surprise, surprise!*, *Just what you might expect.*); Indifference (*So what!*, *I don't care*, *all the same to me*, *couldn't care less*); Enquiring about indifference (*Don't you care?, Is it nothing to you that...?*); Fatigue/Resignation (*I'm tired.*, *I've had enough.*, *I'm worn out.*, *Leave it.*); Gratitude (*Thank you so much.*, *Many thanks*, *most kind of you*, *I'm very grateful*); Reacting to gratitude (*Thank you.*, *Not at all.*, *It's been a pleasure.*).
* **2.6 Expressing Moral Attitudes**:
  * Moral obligation (*not supposed to*, *should(n't)*, *ought (not) to*, *It is wrong to cheat*, *Eating people is wrong.*); Approval (*very good*, *fine, excellent!*, *Well done!*, *strongly approve of*, *quite right to object*); Disapproval/Protest (*Tut-tut.*, *Bad show.*, *ought not to have done it*, *shouldn't have done it*, *ashamed of yourself*, *strongly disapprove*, *against/not in favour of*, *Shame!*, *I must protest against*); Enquiring about approval (*Is this OK?, Do you approve of smoking?*); Attaching blame (*It's all my fault.*, *You are to blame for...*); Denying blame (*It wasn't my fault.*, *not to blame*, *entirely innocent*); Apologising/Forgiveness (*I'm so sorry for...*, *Please forgive me*, *I do apologise*); Disturbing someone (*I beg your pardon.*, *Excuse me, please.*); Accepting apology (*Not at all.*, *That's quite all right.*, *It doesn't matter.*, *Forget it.*, *No problem.*, *Let bygones be bygones.*).

## 3. Deciding on and Managing Courses of Action: Suasion
* **3.1 Suggesting joint action**: *Let's + VP!*, *Shall we...?*, *We could/might (perhaps)*, *What/How about...?, Why not...?, Why don't we...?*
* **3.2 Agreeing to a suggestion**: *Yes, let's.*, *Why not, indeed?*, *That's a very good idea.*, *Certainly.*
* **3.3 Requesting action**: Urgent (*Stop!, Don't move!, You must stop.*); Instructions/Orders (*Add flour and stir*, *You turn right*, *We shall meet*, *Will you stop talking now, please*, *Do stop talking, will you?*); Ordering goods (*I'd like..., please*); Asking for something (*I'd very much like...*, *Please may I have...?*, *Can/could I have...?*); Polite requests (*Please sit down.*, *Would/can/could you...?, Would you mind...?*, *I wonder if you could...*); Hints (*It's cold in here, isn't it?*, *It would be nice if someone could shut the window.*); Pleading (*Please don't hurt me.*); Assistance (*Help!*, *Could you help me, please?*, *Do you think you could give me a hand?*).
* **3.4 Responding to requests**: Willingly (*Certainly.*, *Willingly.*, *With pleasure.*, *It's a pleasure.*); With reservations (*Yes, if I can.*, *Only if...*, *Not unless...*); Reluctance (*Well, all right.*, *If you like.*, *If I must.*, *If you insist.*, *I suppose I'd better.*); Demurring (*I don't really know if I can.*, *I'd like to, but...*, *I'll see what I can do.*, *I can't promise.*); Refusing (*No way!*, *Sorry.*, *Out of the question.*, *Not a hope.*, *I'm afraid it's impossible.*); Defiance (*Oh no I won't!*, *Never!*, *Oh yes I will!*, *Just you try to stop me!*, *Over my dead body!*).
* **3.5 Offering assistance**: *Let me help you.*, *Can I help you?*, *Can I do anything to help?*, *Can I be of assistance?*, *What can I do for you?*, *Can you manage?*
* **3.6 Giving advice**: *You should...*, *You really ought to...*, *Why don't you...?*, *If I were you I'd...*, *You could always try sending a fax.*
* **3.7 Giving warnings**: *Be careful!*, *Look out!*, *Watch out for...*, *Mind your head!*, *That knife is sharp!*
* **3.8 Giving encouragement**: *Come on!*, *Now then*, *Keep it up!*, *Keep on trying.*
* **3.9 Asking permission**: *Can/could/may/might I...?, Do you mind if I...?, Is it all right if I...?*, *Do you think I could go now, please?*
* **3.10 Granting permission**: Willingly (*Yes.*, *Certainly.*, *Please do.*, *By all means.*, *Of course.*, *That's quite all right by me.*); With reservations (*Yes*, *If you're quick about it.*, *Provided you don't stay out too late.*, *Not unless...*); Reluctance (*If you must.*, *I suppose so.*, *I can't stop you.*, *If you like*).
* **3.11 Refusing permission**: *No, you can't.*, *I'm afraid not.*, *I'm sorry, but it's not allowed.*, *Not here until 6.30 p.m.*
* **3.12 Prohibiting**: *Don't...*, *You can't...*, *Don't you dare...!, I forbid you to...*, *I cannot allow you to...*, *I must veto this proposal.*
* **3.13 Offering to do something**: *Can I do anything for you?*, *Can/Shall I... (for you)?, Would you like me to...?*; Pressing (*Do let me carry that case.*, *You must let me...*).
* **3.14 Offering something**: *Would you like...?*, *Let me offer you a lift.*, *Do have another.*, *How about...?*
* **3.15 Inviting**: *How would you like to...?, What about...?*; Pressing (*Do spend a weekend at our place.*, *You simply must...*); Tentative (*You wouldn't like to..., would you?*, *I don't suppose you'd like to...*).
* **3.16 Declining offer/invitation**: Firm (*No, thank you.*, *I'm sorry but I can't.*, *It's very kind of you, but...*, *Unfortunately I can't*, *I'm afraid I can't*); Demurring (*I don't think I really ought to.*, *I don't want to put you to any trouble.*, *Are you sure it's convenient?*, *I don't really know*).
* **3.17 Enquiring if accepted**: *Will you be coming to dinner after all?*, *Do you know yet whether you can come?*

## 4. Socialising
* **4.1 Attracting attention**: *Excuse me.*, *Hallo.*, *I say...*
* **4.2 Greeting people**: Strangers/Acquaintances (*Good morning/afternoon/evening*, *Hallo*); Friends (*How are you?, How are you doing/keeping/getting on?*); Replies (*I'm fine, thank you.*, *Well, so-so.*, *Much better, thank you.*).
* **4.3 Address forms**: Friends/Relatives (*First name*); Acquaintances (*Title + family name*, *Mr/Mrs/Miss + family name*, *Sir + first name*); Strangers (*Professor/Doctor/Officer/Captain*, *Sir/madam*); Endearment (*My dear/darling/love*).
* **4.4 Making introductions**: Formal (*Professor Smith, may I introduce Dr Anthony Brown?*, *Mrs Alexander, I'd like you to meet Jonathan Prior.*); Informal (*Jane, this is John Hargreaves.*, *Jenny, meet Bill: Bill, meet Jenny.*); Oneself (*How do you do? My name is James Scott.*, *Hallo, I'm Ted.*); Responses (*How do you do.*, *Hallo.*, *It's good/Pleased to meet you.*); Enquiries (*Do you know each other?, Have you already met?*, *I think you two know each other*).
* **4.5 Welcome**: *Welcome!*, *Do come in.*, *Make yourself at home.*
* **4.6 At a meal**: No special greeting before eating; *Please help yourself.*
* **4.7 Proposing a toast**: *Cheers!*, *Your very good health.*, *Here's to the bride and groom!*
* **4.8 Congratulating**: *Many congratulations!*, *Well done!*, *Brilliant!*, *Well run/played!*
* **4.9 Good wishes**: Birthday (*Many happy returns of the day!*, *Happy birthday!*); Festivals (*Merry Christmas!*, *Happy New Year!*); Success (*Best of luck!*, *Hope it all goes well.*, *Cross fingers!*); Outings/Holidays (*Enjoy yourself!, Have a good time!*); Parting (*All the best!*, *Keep well.*, *Look after yourself.*, *Take care.*, *Do keep in touch.*, *Let's be hearing from you.*, *Have a good journey/trip/holiday.*).
* **4.10 Taking leave**: Formal (*Good morning/afternoon/evening/night* with low rising intonation); Informal (*It's been nice meeting you.*, *See you again soon.*, *See you next week.*, *Goodbye for now.*); Colloquial (*So long.*, *Bye-bye!*, *Cheerio!*); Final (*Goodbye.*).

## 5. Structuring Discourse
* **5.1 Opening**: Formal (*Ladies and gentlemen!*, *Mr/Madam Chairman*, *Chair(person)*); Informal (*Right (now)!*, *Well (now)*, *Ahem*).
* **5.2 Introducing a theme/topic**: *To begin with, I'd like to + VPinf*, *Now, I'd like to say something about + NP*, *I'd like to tell you about...*, *I'd like to tell you what happened...*, *There was this NP...*
* **5.3 Expressing an opinion**: *As I see it...*, *In my opinion...*, *Personally I think...*, *If you ask me...*
* **5.4 Enumerating**: *In the first place... in the second place...*, *First... second(ly)... third(ly)*, *First... then... then...*, *and... and...*, *For one thing... for another...*
* **5.5 Exemplifying**: *for example*, *e.g.*, *for instance*, *etc.*, *and so on*.
* **5.6 Emphasising**: Speech (*Strong stress*, *high falling nucleus*, *fall-rise in subordinate groups*); Writing (*NB*, *underlining*, *italics*, *bolding*, *capitals*, *especially/extremely/particularly/very*, *Please note that...*, *It is important to...*, *I must emphasize...*).
* **5.7 Defining**: *that is to say*, *i.e.*
* **5.8 Summarising**: *to sum up*, *in brief*, *to cut a long story short*, *all in all*.
* **5.9 Changing theme**: *To change the subject...*, *Now for something different.*, *To pass on to something else...*, *I have another point to make.*
* **5.10 Asking someone to pass to a new theme**: *I'd like to ask you something else.*
* **5.11 Asking opinion**: *What do you think about...?, What is your view?, Where do you stand on...?, How do you see it?*
* **5.12 Showing one is following**: *I see.*, *Yes/No*, *Uh-huh*, *Really?*, *Oh!*, *Indeed.*, *Is that so?*, *How interesting.*
* **5.13 Interrupting/asking floor**: *Excuse me.*, *May I come in here?*, *May I say something?*, *One moment, please.*
* **5.14 Objecting/protesting**: *No, I'm sorry but...*, *I strongly object!*, *That is quite unacceptable.*, *I can't let you get away with that.*
* **5.15 Silence**: *Sh!*, *Quiet, please.*, *Shut up!*, *Pipe down!*
* **5.16 Wish to continue**: *Just one moment, please.*, *Please let me finish.*, *As I was saying...*
* **5.17 Encouraging to continue**: *Do go on, please.*
* **5.18 Coming to an end**: *Finally...*, *To finish, I should like to say...*
* **5.19 Closing**: Speech (*Thank you for your attention.*); Conversation (*Well, it's been nice talking to you.*).
* **5.20 Using the telephone**: Answering (*Giving number*, *Hallo, this is Mary speaking.*); Initiator (*Marcel here.*, *Hallo, is that Berkshire Motors?*); Asking for person (*Can I speak to... please?*, *Could you put me through to...?, Extension 106, please.*); Verifying (*Who is that calling, please?*); Waiting (*Hold the line, please.*, *The line is engaged.*); Audio check (*Are you still there?, Can you hear me?*); New call (*I'll call back later.*); Signing off (*I must go now.*, *Thank you for calling.*).
* **5.21 Letters**: Opening (*Dear Sir/Madam*, *Dear Professor Jones*, *Dear Dick*, *My dear Harry*, *My dearest Helen*, *Darling George*); Closing (*Yours faithfully + signature*, *Yours sincerely*, *With all best wishes*, *Yours ever*, *With love*).

## 6. Assuring and Repairing Communication
* **6.1 Signalled non-understanding**: *Sorry?*, *I don't quite understand (that word / expression / what you said).*
* **6.2 Asking for repetition**: Whole utterance (*I beg your pardon?*, *What did you say, please?*, *Could you say that again, please?*, *Could you repeat that?*); Particular word (*Sorry, where does he live?, What did you say his name was?*).
* **6.3 Asking for confirmation**: Text (*Did you say X?*, *Did you say X or Y?*); Understanding (*Do you mean to say that...?*).
* **6.4 Asking for definition/clarification**: *What does X mean?*, *What do you mean by X?*, *What is X exactly?*, *Could you explain that for me, please?*
* **6.5 Asking someone to spell something**: *Could you spell that word, please?*, *How do you spell that?*
* **6.6 Asking for something to be written down**: *Could you write that down for me, please?*, *Do you mind writing that down?*
* **6.7 Expressing ignorance of the word required**: *I don't know how to say/express it.*, *I don't know what you call it.*, *I don't know the word in English.*, *In German we say X.*
* **6.8 Appeal for assistance in finding an expression**: *What is the English for X?*, *How do you say X in English?*
* **6.9 Filling hesitation pauses**: *...er*, *...you know*, *now let me think...*, *just a moment...*, *what's the word for it...*, *how shall I put it...*
* **6.10 Substituting for a forgotten noun/name**: Meaningless nouns (*what's-his-name*, *what-do-you-call-it*, *thingummyjig*); Paraphrasing (*a kind/sort of generic term*, *something like + related term*, *something + relative clause*).
* **6.11 Asking someone to speak more slowly**: *Could you speak a little more slowly, please.*, *Not so fast, please.*
* **6.12 What to do if interlocutor does not understand**: Repeating (*X repeated directly / spoken more slowly*); *I said X*, *What I said was X*, *I told/asked you to + VPinf*, *I asked you + indirect question*; Spelling out (*pronouncing letter names*, *X is spelt...*, *You spell it...*); Correcting oneself (*Sorry, no, not X, Y!*, *I meant Y, not X*, *...or rather...*, *That's not exactly what I meant.*, *Let me start again.*).
* **6.13 Asking if understood**: *Is that clear now?*, *Do you understand now?*
* **6.14 Supplying a word/expression**: *Do(n't) you mean X?*, *Perhaps you mean X.*, *I think you probably mean X.*, *X perhaps.*
