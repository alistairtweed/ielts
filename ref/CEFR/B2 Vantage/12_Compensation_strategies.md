# 12 Compensation strategies

No matter how hard we try to assess and to predict learners’ communication needs, there will always be a very broad margin of unpredictability, alongside failures of recall on the spur of the moment. What may be expected of learners at Vantage level, then, is skill in dealing with the demands of a communication situation that they are not fully prepared for.

## I As a reader, the learner can:
1. deduce the meanings of complex words composed of familiar elements (base and affixes) combined in accordance with productive rules of word formation;
2. deduce the meanings of unfamiliar elements (phrases and words) from a context of familiar elements;
3. correctly interpret the meanings of 'international words' that are familiar from the native language, while remaining on the alert for 'false friends';
4. find the meanings of unknown words or phrases in bilingual as well as in monolingual dictionaries;
5. derive specific information from a text containing unknown elements.

## II As a listener, the learner can:
1. carry out regular word-formation deductions from speech, provided phonological changes are regular;
2. deduce meanings from linear contextual clues without necessitating heavy backtracking;
3. interpret spoken international words based on standard phonological correspondences;
4. derive specific information from a text containing unknown elements;
5. in face-to-face contacts, appeal to a communication partner's assistance (using the mechanisms in Language Functions section 6).

## III As a speaker, the learner can:
1. introduce a rephrasing: *(Sorry,) I’ll start again.*, *(Sorry,) I’ll try to say that again.*
2. describe by means of paraphrase, using a general word or superordinate with qualifications of physical properties (colour, size, shape), specific features, or use;
3. describe by referring to qualities and properties: *It is...*, *It has...*, *You can... with it.*
4. identify by indicating: *I’d like this, please.*, *I mean the one over there.*
5. appeal for assistance: *What do you call that (again)?, I don’t know the English word. In [native language] we say...*
6. use paralinguistic means of communication (mime, gesture, facial expression, etc.)

## IV As a writer, the learner can:
1. express ignorance: *I don’t know how to say it.*, *I don’t know what you call it.*
2. use paraphrase and properties description;
3. use dictionaries, both bilingual and monolingual.

## V As a social agent, the learner can:
1. apologise for uncertainty or ignorance as to the accepted code of behaviour: *I’m sorry, I don’t/didn't know...*
2. refer to what is customary in his/her own country: *In my country...*
3. ask for guidance: *How is this done in your country?, How should I do this?, What should I do?*
