# 15 By-products

In addition to enabling learners to satisfy their estimated communication needs, a course designed for Vantage will inevitably do other things as well. Some of these things, such as 'learning to learn' (Chapter 13) or 'compensation strategies' (Chapter 12), are deliberately planned for. Other things automatically follow from successful learning experiences without explicit provisions or mentions in the objective description. These are regarded as 'by-products' of a successful learning experience. This chapter briefly discusses two such by-products: literary appreciation and mediation skill.

## Literary appreciation
By 'literary appreciation' we mean here 'the ability to understand literary products and to experience – possibly even to evaluate – their impacts'. In the objective specifications for Vantage, notably in Chapter 9 (Dealing with texts), nothing is explicitly said about literary texts, nor are they excluded. In fact, numerous literary texts easily satisfy all the generic criteria established for text delimitation.

Thus, it may be expected that at Vantage level the learners' general command of English is such as to enable them to read, and enjoy, many short stories, novels, poems, etc., even if they have not been specifically trained or prepared for the reading of literary works. While a course incorporating works of literature can provide richer and more rewarding stimuli and improve vocabulary retention, such specific structural experiences are not explicitly demanded by the core objective, remaining a beneficial by-product of general Vantage competence.

## Mediation skill
'Mediation' is the term used for those language activities where 'the language user is not concerned to express his/her own meanings, but simply to act as an intermediary between interlocutors who are unable to understand each other directly, normally (but not exclusively) speakers of different languages'. Mediation may involve such highly professionalised activities as simultaneous interpretation, consecutive interpretation, exact technical translation, or literary translation, which require intensive specialized study.

However, a certain degree of useful mediation skill will naturally develop as a by-product of learning for Vantage level, especially the type indicated as **'informal interpretation'**. This involves informal interpretation:
* for friends, family, clients, or foreign guests;
* of foreign visitors in one's own country;
* of native speakers when abroad;
* in everyday social and transactional situations;
* of signs, menus, notices, and simple printed instructions.

Such language activities will often take the form of an immediate or summary response to queries such as:
* *What did he/she say?*
* *What does it say here/there?*
* *Tell him/her that...*
* *Ask him/her if...*

The foreign language ability of learners at Vantage level is such that responses of this kind are well within their capabilities, as is summarizing the gist of newspaper and magazine articles for the benefit of someone unfamiliar with the target language. The acquisition of this informal interpretation skill does not depend on separate instructional frameworks or testing evaluation; it is simply a natural by-product of reaching Vantage level proficiency.
